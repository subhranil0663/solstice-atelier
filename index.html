Sculpted elliptical focus prevents flutter echo

By replacing parallel planar surfaces with mathematically calculated true ellipses, sound energy travels along conjugate reflection axes. Standing wave nodes converge into controlled dispersion focal points rather than trapped room flutter.

  • Dual geometric focal nodes eliminate slapback echoes without deadening high frequencies.
  • Four peripheral hand-cast borosilicate optical lenses scatter upper harmonic treble evenly.
  • Concealed corner basalt cavities absorb low-frequency rumble below sixty hertz.

Concave glass prisms disperse high frequency reflections

Built into the granite foothills of northern Kyoto, the Kurama Sanctuary couples deep subterranean rock mass with an overhead canopy of suspended cast-glass acoustic prisms, achieving natural decay times tailored for chamber music and solo voice.

  • Heavy basalt foundation isolates exterior seismic and urban noise interference.
  • Suspended optical glass prism canopy redirects specular reflections toward acoustic baffles.
  • Linear reverberation decay calibrated to half a second across all musical registers.

Sound is architecture experienced through time

Traditional recording and listening environments rely heavily on porous foam or fabric panels to suffocate unwanted reflections. In stripping away resonance, these spaces introduce a quiet claustrophobia that separates the listener from physical presence.

When sound waves encounter sculpted cast glass, acoustic reflection becomes an architectural dialogue rather than an engineering compromise.

In Kyoto, our studio approaches room acoustics through the physical properties of heavy natural mass and optical refraction. By pairing solid subterranean basalt foundations with hand-cast borosilicate glass diffusers, high frequencies are dispersed with crystal clarity while lower octaves breathe naturally. The result is a room that feels simultaneously luminous and acoustically serene.
